# REVIEW-2026-09 — the external design review, adjudicated

An external reviewer (a different model, "Fable") was given the repo and produced a design
review. This document **adjudicates it against the code and the measurement archive** — what is
true, what is wrong, and what both the review and twelve programs missed.

**Read this before quoting the review.** Its headline numbers are accurate to the decimal — they
were derived from `docs/measurements/`, not guessed — but four of its structural claims are wrong
in ways that change what to build, and the largest finding available in this repository is not in
it.

Base commit: `74b0e5a`. Every number below was re-derived on this tree; none is quoted from a doc.
Method for the archive figures: pooled over the 320 chunks of `docs/measurements/p24/`
(6,400 campaigns, 3,200 CRN-paired greedy/sloppy campaign pairs).

---

## 1. THE FINDING THIS REPOSITORY ALREADY OWNED AND NEVER READ

### Playing well and playing badly win equally often.

The flywheel runs two policies on **the same worlds** (common random numbers): `greedy` plays
well; `sloppy` forgets overwatch ~15% of the time, settles for a mediocre tile within a −40 score
band ~15% of the time, and overextends ~10% of the time (`Game.Autopilot.cs` `SloppyDest`, `Slip`).

Pooled over **3,200 CRN-paired campaigns**:

| | greedy | sloppy | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| campaign win rate | **29.0%** | **28.7%** | **+0.2** |
| missions cleared | 3.83 | 3.79 | +0.04 |
| run turns | 25.0 | 24.8 | +0.2 |
| discordant worlds | — | — | 770 / 3,200 = **24.1%** (389 greedy-only, 381 sloppy-only) |

Per rung, both arms of the P24 round:

| arm | RECRUIT | h0 | h2 | h4 | h6 | h8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| aim | **−5.6** | +0.3 | **−7.5** | −2.2 | +1.2 | +2.8 |
| base | **−4.4** | +2.8 | **−3.1** | +7.2 | −1.9 | +2.5 |

**At the two rungs a new player actually meets, the sloppy bot is AHEAD, in both arms
independently.** And the two policies die in the same places in the same proportions — losing
mission objective shares agree within 0.9 points on all eight objectives.

So: the worlds diverge (24.1% play out differently), and the divergence does not reach the
outcome. **The instrument that drove twelve programs cannot distinguish good play from bad play.**

**The honest caveat, stated plainly:** the sloppy model is *bounded* — it never blunders
catastrophically — so this measures "a 15%-degraded player", not "a bad player". The defensible
claim is therefore: *within the band of play the instrument models, skill is worth 0.2 ± ~3.5
points.* That is still fatal to the programme, because **every balance conclusion in this
repository — the ladder, the bands, every lever — was derived from an instrument that cannot see
the thing the game is for.**

**This was measured, named and then filed.** `docs/DEVLOG.md` records "the aggregate policy gap
sits at ~0 … sloppy viable, flagged to trend" and FUL-13 **accepted** it as forgiving-by-design.
The accept-argument is half right: you must not sharpen the *bot* to manufacture a gap. But the
gap is a property of the **game**, not the bot, and "forgiving" is not a defensible property of
*every* rung including the apex.

### Why: on most objectives, the fight is optional.

Mid-run missions, pooled over the same 6,400 campaigns — force deployed against you vs bodies you
actually kill:

| objective | force | killed | kill% | dmg taken | turns | win% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Evac | 8.73 | 0.66 | **7.6%** | 16.3 | 8.4 | 74.1 |
| Decapitate | 9.74 | 1.21 | 12.5% | 36.4 | 6.2 | 23.1 |
| Rescue | 8.51 | 1.07 | 12.6% | 14.7 | 5.8 | 76.3 |
| Hack | 9.12 | 1.33 | 14.6% | 16.8 | 4.4 | 62.6 |
| Escort | 8.95 | 1.50 | 16.8% | 20.9 | 8.1 | 73.9 |
| Sabotage | 6.59 | 1.60 | 24.2% | 21.0 | 4.8 | 58.5 |
| Defend | 12.34 | 3.61 | 29.2% | 31.8 | 8.5 | 67.6 |
| Eliminate | 8.46 | 3.73 | 44.1% | 36.3 | 8.4 | 27.1 |
| **all mid-run** | | | **23.3%** | | | |

**23.3% of the deployed hostile force is ever killed.** On Evac, nine hostiles deploy, two thirds
of one dies, and you win three times in four. `docs/DESIGN.md` §4 already carried this as
"Engagement mass — Thin" with C3's 25.1% / 3.3% figures; it has been an open scorecard row for
two programs.

If declining the fight is optimal on five of eight objectives, the sloppy bot's edge at low heat
is not a paradox: **the greedy bot fights, and fighting is a losing move.**

## 2. THE MECHANISM — the game deletes its own geometry, exactly where the fight is

This is the part neither the review nor any wave located, and it is cheap to fix.

**Objective sites are literal expressions, identical on every mission of that objective, in every
mode, at every heat, on all 35 arenas:**

| objective | site | source |
|---|---|---|
| HACK terminal | `(Grid.W/2 + 1, Grid.H/2)` → **always (10,5)** | `Game.cs:2137` |
| SABOTAGE | three fixed tiles **(5,3) (10,5) (13,7)** | `Game.cs:2139` |
| EVAC / ESCORT / RESCUE extraction | an 8-tile 2×4 block, **cols 16-17, rows 0-3** | `Game.cs:2124` |
| RESCUE captive | `(Grid.W/2, Grid.H/2)` → **always (9,5)** | `Game.cs:2226` |

**And the arena's terrain is erased around each one.** `Mission.Build` puts every spawn, the whole
evac zone, the terminal + its full 3×3 ring and each sabotage site + its 3×3 ring into an
`occupied` set (`Mission.cs:354-367`), and `TryApplyLayout` does
`if (occupied.Contains((x, y))) continue;` (`Mission.cs:597`).

So CITADEL's bunker, PLAZA's plateau and ZIGGURAT's stepped mound **cannot shape the fight at the
objective, by construction.** The focal point of every mission is the one place on the board
guaranteed to have no terrain.

**Measured consequence.** Across the 35 authored arenas (open-floor share 69.7% → 95.5%), pooled
over 6,400 campaigns:

- `corr(open-floor %, win %)` = **−0.15**
- controlling for mission number (143 arena×mission cells): **−0.153**

**Thirty-five hand-authored boards, and their geometry explains essentially nothing about whether
you win** — because the geometry is deleted where it would have mattered.

serious reading.

### Corrected — these change what to build

**1. "The kill seam takes no attacker today."** *Half right, and the correction makes the fix
cheaper.* `KillUnit(Unit d)` does take one parameter — but the killer is **already computed inside
it**: `Game.cs:3367`
`Unit killer = ActiveAnim switch { ShotAnim sa => sa.A, GrenadeAnim ga => ga.Thrower, _ => null };`
and is then reduced to `killer.Cls`, a class string, for telemetry. Nothing needs threading through
call sites. The chronicle is a matter of **not discarding a local that is already in scope**, at a
site where the victim, the tile and the living neighbours are all also in scope.
`FallenRec` (`Run.cs:225`) is 5 fields: Name, Cls, Rank, Kills, Mission.

**2. "Walls that block sight" is new engine work.** *Wrong — the mechanic already exists.*
`Grid.BlocksSight` (`Grid.cs:240`) returns true for `TileType.HighCover`, and `HasLineOfSight` is
a supercover Bresenham that respects it. **High cover already blocks line of sight.** What is
missing is not the mechanic but the *architecture*: the 35 boards average **12.1 sight-blocking
tiles per 198-tile board**, placed as scattered singletons rather than walls, corners and rooms.
Doubling the sight-blocking mass is a **content change**, not an engine change.
(True edge-based occlusion — doors, windows, a wall *between* two tiles — **is** an engine change,
and the review is right about that half.)

**3. Board size is a hard blocker.** *Stale.* `Grid` already carries instance `W`/`H` and sizes
every one of its eight per-tile arrays from them (`Grid.cs:11-12, 36`). There are **five**
`Cfg.GridW`/`Cfg.GridH` references in all of `src/` — two of them in the harness. The simulation is
dimension-clean. The real costs are elsewhere and are real: all 35 templates fail
`TryApplyLayout`'s dimension check and the game falls back to procedural **silently**; `CamZoom` is
clamped to a minimum of 1.0 so you cannot zoom out; 49 sites in `Renderer.cs` read `Cfg.Tile`; and
every balance number would need re-measuring. June's "NO-GO" verdict was correct then and is
overstated now.

**4. "Verbs from 23 to about 8" / "the cull breaks the enum pins."** `Hud.VerbTable` is **16**
rows. The archive has ever recorded **26** distinct action types, of which **14 occur on under 1%
of actions**, and **SHOVE and VAULT have never been recorded once in the entire 209 MB archive**.
The enum-pin worry is misplaced: arenas and archetypes are not persisted (the arena is dealt from
`MapSeed`), so culling content does not touch the save format. Culling a *persisted enum member*
would — and that is a different list.

### The reviewer's own ordering problem

Item 0 is "put a human in front of it first — this is the precondition, not a footnote … everything
below is my bet." Item 1 is then a specified, sequenced rewrite that would void the measurement
archive and the 35 arenas. If the witness is genuinely the precondition, the build order cannot be
settled before it. The review reproduces, at one remove, the failure it correctly diagnoses:
designing confidently without one.

## 4. FURTHER DEFECTS FOUND DURING ADJUDICATION

**a. Shoot-and-hold is legal; the game's sharpest binary choice is gone.**
`Unit.BeginTurn` sets `ActionsLeft = 2`. A shot costs **1** (`Game.cs:6045`). `DoOverwatch`
(`Game.cs:6092`) gates only on `!CanAct || Ammo <= 0` — there is **no `FiredThisTurn` check** — and
then sets `ActionsLeft = 0`. So a soldier may fire **and** hold a reaction on the same turn.
Consequence nobody has recorded: `IssueGrenade`/`IssueItem` also set `ActionsLeft = 0`, so **a
grenade costs two actions if thrown first and one if thrown second** — identical intent, different
price. *Whether the line is dominant is a separate question and is not established here: the bot
sets overwatch on 4.10% of actions.* The **exclusivity** is what is gone, and it is not recorded
anywhere.

**b. `Ai.Tier`, the heat ladder's "qualitative tooth", is nearly inert** — four constants, two of
them bypassed whenever a SPOTTER is alive, and at Tier 2 one is an exact no-op against the SPOTTER
branch. This is C1's dead-row class again, one layer down.

**c. The autopilot has no target-priority entry for twelve of ~21 archetypes**, including ones
whose design brief is "kill it first" — they score as a GRUNT. **Every balance number in this
project was produced by a bot that cannot tell them apart.**

**d. Mission 1 is always the same encounter class** — forced Eliminate, forced no-faction, and its
force can contain only SCOUT and GRUNT. It is also the mission that ends roughly a quarter of all
runs.

**e. (FIXED, `1a1414c`) The FIELD MANUAL documented four perks the game cannot give**
(Deadeye/Opportunist/PointBlank/Vanguard — retired from `PerkDef.All`, unimplemented in
`Combat.cs`), because `CODEXTEST` asserted every perk *has* copy rather than that the copy
describes something reachable.

**f. (FIXED, `1a1414c`) The max-pressure banner announced reinforcements that cannot arrive** on
ELIMINATE, where C3 deliberately disabled that arm.

## 5. WHAT FOLLOWS

Ordered by evidence, not by ambition.

1. **Arenas own their objective sites and spawns** (§2). The cheapest change with the largest
   reach: it is a template-legend parse plus a build-order inversion, it touches no save format,
   and it is the precondition for *any* claim that board geometry matters. Shipping as wave **P26**.
2. **Make "does skill matter" measurable.** `byArena`/`byObjective` are not split by policy, so the
   central question cannot be read out of a chunk. Measurement-only; campaign-inert by construction.
3. **Then, and only then, price bigger boards.** With sites owned by arenas and the policy gap
   readable per board, the big-map prototype becomes a measurable question rather than a bet.
4. **The squad chronicle** (§3, correction 1) — cheap, additive, no measurement risk.
5. **Retire bot win rate as the objective function.** Not because it cannot see fun, but because
   §1 shows it cannot see *play*.

**Open and owner-only:** a human session. `Stats` is never enabled outside the measurement harness,
so no human session has ever produced a single byte of telemetry. Until one does, everything above
is inference from a bot.
